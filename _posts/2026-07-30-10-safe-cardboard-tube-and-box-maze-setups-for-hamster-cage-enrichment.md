Hamsters are natural burrowers and navigators that can travel several miles every night in search of food. Building cardboard mazes out of tissue boxes, paper towel rolls, and shoe boxes is one of the easiest ways to provide mental stimulation without spending money. However, safety comes first when crafting for small pets. Always strip away tape, shipping labels, and metallic staples before building. Ensure all exit holes are at least 3 inches (7.5 cm) in diameter for [Syrian hamsters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_hamster) and 2 inches (5 cm) for dwarf species so they never get stuck while carrying food in their cheek pouches. For assembly, stick to non-toxic options like plain [Elmer's](https://www.elmers.com) school glue or a simple flour-and-water paste.

Here are 10 safe, tested cardboard tube and box setups to enrich your hamster's cage or playpen.

## 1. The Shoe Box Labyrinth
Take a standard cardboard shoe box and glue cut cardboard strips inside to form 3 or 4 distinct corridors. Cut 3-inch archways between the internal walls so your hamster can move through the interior. Hide small clusters of seeds or sprays in dead ends to turn it into a foraging puzzle.

## 2. Sub-Bedding Tunnel Network
Connect 3 or 4 paper towel tubes end-to-end by cutting small 1-inch slits into the ends and slotting them together. Lay the network flat on the cage floor and cover it with 4 to 6 inches of paper or aspen bedding, leaving only the two open ends exposed above the substrate as entrance points.

## 3. Stacked Tissue Box Tower
Remove any plastic film from two square tissue boxes. Glue one box on top of the other. Cut a hole in the top of the lower box and the bottom of the upper box, then insert a cardboard tube diagonally through the floor to act as an internal ladder.

## 4. The Modular Tea-Box Grid
Save 4 to 6 small tea or snack boxes. Cut connecting cutouts into the side walls where the boxes touch, then glue them into a 2x2 or 2x3 grid. Leave 2 outer holes open as entryways and drop a pinch of bedding and a few oats into each chamber.

## 5. The Vertical Zig-Zag Ramp Box
Stand an empty cereal box upright. Glue 3 alternating diagonal cardboard shelves inside to create a climbing path. Cut a bottom entrance hole and a top exit hole, allowing your hamster to climb up the internal ramps safely.

## 6. The Egg Carton Forage Tunnel
Cut an access hole into the side of an egg carton made from unbleached paper pulp. Connect a paper towel tube into this hole. Place a few sunflower seeds into the egg cups and close the lid loosely so your hamster has to nudge the carton open after navigating the entrance tube.

## 7. Peek-a-Boo Underground Tube
Take a thick 12-inch cardboard mailing tube. Cut three 3-inch skylight holes along one side of the tube. Place it horizontally on the cage floor and pack bedding around it so that only the top cutout windows poke out, creating a underground highway with multiple escape hatches.

## 8. The T-Junction Hideout
Take three short paper rolls. Cut a round hole into the center side of one tube and insert the second tube inside to form a "T" shape. Glue a third tube to the opposite side to make a four-way junction. Place this junction inside a nesting area to give your hamster multiple flight paths.

## 9. Open-Top Playpen Labyrinth
Lay a shallow parcel box flat and cut off the top flaps. Glue internal maze dividers to the bottom, keeping all paths at least 3.5 inches wide. Use this exclusively during supervised playpen time outside the cage, allowing you to watch your hamster solve the maze from above.

## 10. Gentle Incline Hanging Ramp
Angle a thick cardboard tube from the cage floor to a low wooden platform. Secure the base into deep bedding and notch the upper end so it rests firmly on the ledge. Ensure the incline stays below 30 degrees so your hamster can walk up easily without slipping.

## Safety and Maintenance
Cardboard enrichment is temporary by design. Inspect these setups daily during spot cleans. Throw away any boxes or tubes that become damp with urine, heavily soiled, or chewed down to sharp edges. Stick to plain, uncoated corrugated cardboard and unbleached paper tubes whenever possible to keep your pet's stomach safe from synthetic dyes and gloss coatings.
