If your fiddle leaf fig is shedding its bottom leaves between November and February, you are not alone. Winter is notoriously difficult for [Ficus lyrata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ficus_lyrata), a species native to the warm, humid lowland rainforests of West Africa. When days grow shorter and indoor heating turns on, the plant enters a semi-dormant state and tries to balance its energy budget.

Lower leaves are usually the first to go because they receive the least light. When a leaf consumes more energy than it produces through photosynthesis, the plant systematically reabsorbs mobile nutrients from that leaf and drops it. Here is why this happens in winter and how to adjust your routine to stop the loss.

## 1. Shorter Days and Weaker Sunlight

In summer, a fiddle leaf fig sitting three feet from an east- or west-facing window gets plenty of indirect sun. In winter, the sun sits lower on the horizon, cloud cover increases, and total daylight hours decrease significantly. As light levels drop, the plant simply cannot support a full canopy of large leaves.

**How to adjust:**
* Move your plant directly in front of your brightest window (south- or west-facing is ideal in winter).
* Wipe dust off the foliage with a damp cloth every two weeks so the leaves can absorb as much light as possible.
* If natural light is limited, add a full-spectrum LED grow bulb positioned 12 to 18 inches above the top canopy for 8 to 10 hours daily.

## 2. Soil Staying Wet for Too Long

Because the plant’s growth slows down and low temperatures reduce evaporation, the soil takes twice as long to dry out in winter. If you keep watering on your summer schedule (such as once a week), the lower roots will sit in saturated, oxygen-depleted soil. This suffocates root hairs, triggering sudden yellowing and leaf drop at the bottom of the stem.

**How to adjust:**
* Never water on a calendar schedule. Insert a wooden chopstick or your finger 2 to 3 inches into the potting mix. Only water when the top half of the pot feels completely dry.
* Always empty the drip tray 15 minutes after watering so the pot never sits in standing water.
* Stop all fertilizer until spring. Feeding a dormant plant in winter can burn sensitive roots and cause additional foliage drop.

## 3. Cold Drafts and Sudden Temperature Swings

Fiddle leaf figs thrive in temperatures between 65°F and 80°F (18°C to 27°C). If temperatures consistently dip below 55°F (13°C), the plant experiences cold shock. A cold draft from a single drafty window frame or an exterior door opening repeatedly is enough to trigger localized leaf drop on the side facing the cold source.

**How to adjust:**
* Keep the plant at least 3 to 4 feet away from exterior doorways, single-pane window glass, and uninsulated walls.
* Keep it equally far from forced-air heating vents and radiators. Hot, dry air blasting directly against the foliage rapidly dehydrates leaves, causing brown edges and premature shedding.

## 4. Plummeting Indoor Humidity

Winter heating systems regularly pull indoor [relative humidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_humidity) down to 15% to 25%, while a fiddle leaf fig prefers 40% to 60%. While low humidity alone rarely causes healthy green leaves to fall, it dries out leaf edges and compounds the stress caused by low light and watering errors.

**How to adjust:**
* Run a small cool-mist humidifier near your plant to maintain ambient moisture above 40%.
* Group your fiddle leaf fig with other houseplants to create a microclimate with naturally higher humidity.
* Skip misting with a spray bottle; it raises humidity for only a few minutes and can promote fungal leaf spot if water lingers on the leaves.

## Winter Care Checklist

| Factor | Summer Routine | Winter Adjustment |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Light** | Bright indirect, pulled back from glass | Direct winter sun / right against bright window |
| **Watering** | When top 1–2 inches are dry | When top half of the pot is completely dry |
| **Fertilizer** | Monthly with balanced houseplant feed | None until new spring growth appears |
| **Location** | Away from strong AC drafts | Away from cold drafts and heating vents |
