Staring at a blank farewell card passed around the office or linked in an email can feel surprisingly tough. You want to sound genuine without writing a novel, and you definitely want to avoid defaulting to a generic "Best of luck!" that looks like you spent three seconds typing it.

The secret to writing a great farewell message is matching your tone to your actual relationship with the person who is leaving. A message to a manager who mentored your career growth should sound different from a message to a teammate you shared daily coffee breaks and inside jokes with.

Here are 20 concrete examples you can copy, tweak, or combine, categorized by your relationship with the departing employee.

## 10 Farewell Card Messages for Managers and Senior Leaders

When writing to a manager, focus on gratitude, specific guidance they provided, or how their leadership impacted your career. Even in a casual company culture, keeping a respectful and appreciative tone is best.

1. "Thank you for being such an encouraging manager over the last three years. Your guidance during the Q3 platform redesign made all the difference for our team."
2. "I’ve learned so much from watching how you handle high-pressure projects. Thank you for your patience and [mentorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mentorship)."
3. "Thank you for always advocating for our department and giving me room to grow into my role. Best of luck with your next adventure!"
4. "Your leadership set a high standard for how our team communicates and supports each other. You will be missed!"
5. "Thank you for always keeping your door open and helping me navigate my first year at the company. I wish you the absolute best."
6. "Working under your management has been a highlight of my time here. Thank you for always trusting my judgment on client accounts."
7. "Your vision and steady direction kept our team grounded through every reorg. Thank you for everything you’ve done for us."
8. "Thank you for encouraging me to take on new responsibilities. I wouldn't have reached my current goal without your backing."
9. "It has been a privilege learning from you over the past two years. I hope our paths cross again professionally!"
10. "Thank you for building such a positive, resilient team culture. Wishing you every success in your new leadership role."

## 10 Farewell Card Messages for Close Colleagues and Work Friends

For peers, desk neighbors, or close friends, you can be warm, personal, and conversational. Feel free to reference specific shared experiences, inside jokes, or future plans to stay connected.

11. "Work is going to be about 80% less entertaining without our 10:00 AM coffee runs. Don't be a stranger!"
12. "I don't know who I'm going to turn to for quick sanity checks during long meetings now. I'm going to miss working with you!"
13. "So excited for your new role, but selfishly sad you're leaving! Let's make sure to get dinner next month to catch up."
14. "Thanks for making every crazy launch deadline bearable over the past two years. They are lucky to have you at your new spot."
15. "You were the absolute best desk neighbor I could have asked for. Keep in touch on LinkedIn and text!"
16. "I am officially holding a small grudge against your new company for stealing you away, but I wish you the absolute best!"
17. "From panicked pitch preps to late-night Slack chats, thanks for being in my corner. Don't forget us little people!"
18. "Who is going to translate complicated spreadsheet formulas for me now? Thank you for all your help and friendship."
19. "Beyond grateful for your help on the team and your friendship outside of work. Let's keep our regular catch-ups going!"
20. "You made coming into the office genuinely fun. Congratulations on the new job—they have no idea how good they just got."

## Three Rules for Writing a Memorable Goodbye Note

If you want to write your own custom message instead of using a template, follow this basic 3-step structure:

- **Be specific:** Mention a real project, habit, or trait (e.g., "your help with the quarterly reports" instead of "all your hard work").
- **Keep it positive:** Never use a farewell card to voice complaints about management, low pay, or why the person is choosing to leave.
- **Include contact info (if appropriate):** If you want to keep in touch with a close peer, drop your personal phone number or mention expanding your [professional network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_network) on LinkedIn so they know you genuinely want to stay connected.
