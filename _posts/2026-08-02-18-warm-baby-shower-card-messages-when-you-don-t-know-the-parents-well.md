Staring at a blank card for a coworker, neighbor, or distant relative can feel surprisingly tough. You want to sound genuinely happy for the expectant parents, but writing overly emotional declarations when you barely know them can feel awkward or performative.

The secret to writing a great baby shower card to an acquaintance is striking the right balance: polite, encouraging, and warm, without trying to sound like a lifelong best friend. Here are 18 straightforward, thoughtful card messages you can use, grouped by tone and context.

## Short & Sweet Messages
These quick, timeless options work well for almost anyone, whether you are signing a group card or handing over a small gift.

1. **"Wishing your growing family all the best during this exciting new chapter!"** Simple, universally applicable, and perfectly polite.
2. **"So thrilled for you both! Sending warm wishes as you prepare to welcome your baby."** Focuses on positive energy without getting overly personal.
3. **"Congratulations on the upcoming arrival! Wishing you health, happiness, and plenty of rest."** Mentioning rest is a sympathetic touch every expecting parent appreciates.
4. **"Sending big congratulations to your family. I can’t wait to see photos of the little one!"** Friendly and upbeat without requiring long follow-up conversations.
5. **"Warmest wishes on your baby-to-be. Hope delivery day goes smoothly and everyone stays healthy."** Focuses strictly on well-wishes for a safe delivery.

## Professional & Coworker Friendly
When attending a workplace shower or contributing to a team card, keep your message professional yet supportive.

6. **"Wishing you all the best as you step away on leave. We’ll miss you around the office!"** A great note for a colleague you work with regularly.
7. **"So excited for you as you begin this new journey! Wishing you a peaceful and restful leave."** Focuses on their upcoming transition away from work.
8. **"Congratulations! Wishing you a wonderful time bonding with your new baby."** Keeps the emphasis on family time.
9. **"Our team is so excited for you! Best wishes for a smooth adjustment to parenthood."** Excellent for group cards signed by multiple coworkers.
10. **"Congratulations on this huge milestone. Wishing your family endless joy as you grow."** Professional, polite, and respectful.

## Warm Messages for Neighbors and Acquaintances
If the parents are new neighbors or casual acquaintances you see occasionally, these options express genuine warmth without overstepping.

11. **"You are going to be wonderful parents. Sending you so much warmth during this special time."** A classic, comforting compliment that feels thoughtful.
12. **"There is so much joy ahead for your family. Thinking of you as you prepare to meet your little one!"** Encouraging and straightforward.
13. **"Wishing you endless sweet moments and cozy cuddle sessions. Congratulations on your baby!"** A sweet sentiment that works for any parent.
14. **"Welcome to the adventure of parenthood! Wishing your family health and happiness every step of the way."** A supportive nod to the big milestone.

## Messages to Pair with a Gift or Book
If you bought an item off their registry on [Target](https://www.target.com) or brought a children's book instead of a card (a popular request at modern [baby showers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baby_shower)), use one of these messages.

15. **"I picked this out off your registry hoping it makes those early weeks a little easier. So happy for you!"** Connects your gift directly to practical support.
16. **"May this book become a favorite bedtime story for years to come. Congratulations!"** Ideal for writing on the inside cover of a picture book.
17. **"A little something to help build out the nursery setup. Wishing you comfort and joy!"** Works well when gifting crib sheets, swaddles, or nursery items.
18. **"Hope this gift brings a bit of extra ease to your routine. Wishing your new family all the best!"** Ideal for practical items like diapers or bath supplies.

## Simple Rules for Writing Your Card
When you don't know the parents well, follow three simple ground rules to keep the card stress-free:

* **Avoid unsolicited advice:** Skip parenting tips, labor stories, or jokes about never sleeping again. Stick to direct encouragement.
* **Keep gender neutral if unsure:** If the parents haven't shared the baby's sex or name, stick to terms like "baby," "little one," or "new arrival."
* **Include your full name:** If you are writing to a coworker or neighbor, sign with your first and last name (or your department, such as "Alex from Accounting") so they don't have to guess who sent the gift when writing thank-you notes.
