If your game group regularly sits between five and ten people, you will eventually end up deciding between [Secret Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secret_Hitler) and [The Resistance: Avalon](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/128882/the-resistance-avalon). Both are heavyweights in the hidden-role genre, but they produce completely different table dynamics. 

One is a rowdy, accusatory bluffing game where bad luck gives bad actors plausible deniability. The other is a tight, analytical puzzle where almost every piece of information is open to logical scrutiny. Picking the right one comes down to how much randomness your group can stomach and the exact number of chairs around your table.

## The Core Mechanic: Hidden Luck vs. Pure Logic

The fundamental difference between these two games comes down to how information enters the room.

In **Secret Hitler**, policy tiles are drawn from a randomized deck of 17 cards (6 Liberal, 11 Fascist). The President draws three, discards one face-down, and hands two to the Chancellor, who plays one. Because of this tile distribution, Liberals will frequently draw three Fascist policies with no good options. This mechanical randomness is a feature, not a bug: it provides built-in cover for Fascists. A Fascist player can pass two red tiles and honestly shrug, "I only drew red." The table can never be 100% mathematically certain whether a player was unlucky or lying.

In **Avalon**, there is zero deck-drawing luck. The leader proposes a team to go on a quest, everyone votes publicly on that team proposal, and the chosen team members secretly play either a Success or Fail card. Good players must play Success; Evil players choose whether to sabotage or blend in. Every vote is public, creating an indisputable record. If a three-person team goes on a quest and a Fail card appears, exactly one or two of those three people is lying. There is no deck RNG to blame—only player choices.

## Group Size Breakdown: Where Each Game Peaked

Both boxes claim to support 5 to 10 players, but their functional sweet spots are not identical.

*   **5 to 6 Players:** **Avalon wins easily.** Secret Hitler at 5 or 6 players alters its core tension: Hitler knows who their fellow Fascist is from turn one. In Avalon, a 5-player game with Merlin and the Assassin is sharp, fast (20-30 minutes), and provides enough role variety to stay deeply replayable.
*   **7 to 8 Players:** **A tie, depending on style.** This is the undisputed sweet spot for both games. In Secret Hitler, 7 players unlocks the blind Hitler mechanic—Hitler does not know who the Fascists are, forcing Fascists to subtly guide Hitler without exposing themselves. In Avalon, 7 or 8 players allows the full roster of legendary roles: Merlin, Percival, Morgana, and Mordred, creating a brilliant web of counter-bluffs.
*   **9 to 10 Players:** **Secret Hitler handles the crowd better.** Avalon at 10 players can bog down into heavy analysis paralysis. Secret Hitler scales better into high-player counts because the presidential powers (investigating party membership, executing players) accelerate the game's pace and keep energy high.

## Tolerance for Chaos and Player Elimination

Secret Hitler introduces an election tracker: if the table rejects three proposed governments in a row, the top policy of the deck automatically passes without debate, and powers trigger at random. Later in the game, the President must literally execute another player, removing them from the table for the remainder of the round. If your group hates player elimination or feels cheated when an unlucky top-deck flips the game, Secret Hitler will frustrate them.

Avalon features no player elimination. Everyone participates in every vote until the final quest or until the Assassin attempts to identify Merlin. However, Avalon requires sustained mental focus. If a player tunes out for two rounds and misses who voted for which rejected proposal, they will struggle to contribute to the deduction. It rewards players who treat social deduction like a logic grid.

## Which One Should You Buy?

**Pick Secret Hitler if:**
*   Your game nights lean casual, social, and loud.
*   You often have 7 to 10 players.
*   Players enjoy high-stakes drama, table talk, and blaming bad card draws.
*   No one minds sitting out for 10 minutes after catching a bullet.

**Pick The Resistance: Avalon if:**
*   Your group prefers tight deduction games with zero randomness.
*   You frequently play with 5 to 7 players.
*   Your table dislikes player elimination.
*   People enjoy tracking voting patterns and piecing together information systematically.
