When a fiddle leaf fig ([Ficus lyrata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ficus_lyrata)) starts shedding foliage, it can feel like a sudden emergency. However, the color and texture of the fallen leaves offer an exact roadmap to the underlying problem. Healthy, fully green leaves falling off point to shock, sudden light shifts, or draft issues. Leaves dropping with dark brown spots or speckled patterns usually indicate watering mistakes, fungal issues, or pests.

Here are six distinct reasons your plant is shedding foliage—divided by leaf appearance—and the exact steps to fix each one.

## Why Your Plant Drops Green Leaves

### 1. Environmental Shock and Temperature Drafts
If your plant drops firm, fully green leaves without any spots or crisp edges, it is reacting to a sudden change in its environment. Fiddle leaf figs prefer high stability. A draft from an air conditioning unit, a cold doorway opened during winter, or moving the pot to a different room can cause the plant to immediately drop lower foliage.
* **Corrective step:** Keep the plant in a location where temperatures remain steadily between 65°F and 75°F (18°C–24°C). Position it at least 4 to 6 feet away from heating vents, AC units, and drafty exterior doors.

### 2. Acute Underwatering and Hydrophobic Soil
While mild underwatering makes leaf tips dry out and turn crispy, severe drought causes shock. When soil gets bone-dry, it pulls away from the inside edge of the pot and becomes hydrophobic (repelling water). Water simply runs down the sides and out the bottom without reaching the root ball, causing the plant to dump bottom green leaves to save energy.
* **Corrective step:** Bottom-water the plant by submerging the bottom third of the nursery pot in a sink or bucket of standing water for 30 to 45 minutes until the topsoil feels damp. Resume regular top-watering whenever the upper 2 inches of soil feel dry to the touch—typically every 7 to 10 days in spring and summer.

### 3. Light Deficit
Fiddle leaf figs require intense light to support their heavy, large leaves. If you place the pot in a dark corner or move it away from a window as seasonal light changes, the plant cannot produce enough energy via photosynthesis to maintain its canopy. It responds by dropping green leaves from the bottom and inside sections.
* **Corrective step:** Place the plant directly in front of an unobstructed east-facing window or 2 to 3 feet back from a south- or west-facing window. Aim for 400 to 800 foot-candles of bright, indirect light daily.

## Why Your Plant Drops Brown Spotted Leaves

### 4. Root Rot from Overwatering
Dark brown to black spots that start in the center of the leaf or at the base—often surrounded by a faint yellow ring—indicate [root rot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root_rot). Excess moisture suffocates the roots, causing anaerobic fungi to decay the root system. As roots die, they lose the ability to transport water, causing leaf tissue to die and drop.
* **Corrective step:** Stop watering immediately. Unpot the plant and inspect the root ball. Trim away any mushy, dark roots with sterile shears. Repot the fig in a well-draining soil mix (a 2:1 ratio of standard potting mix to perlite or coarse pine bark) inside a container with active drainage holes.

### 5. Spider Mite Damage
Small, reddish-brown pinpoint spots scattered across the surface or underside of dropped leaves point to a pest issue, most commonly spider mites. These microscopic pests pierce leaf cells to drink sap. As the damaged cells die, the leaf takes on a brown, speckled appearance before dropping off entirely.
* **Corrective step:** Wipe down both sides of every remaining leaf with a soft, damp cloth. Spray the entire foliage canopy with insecticidal soap or a 1% cold-pressed neem oil solution once a week for three consecutive weeks to kill hatching mites.

### 6. Bacterial Leaf Spot
Unlike fungal root rot, which creates dark, distinct brown patches, bacterial infections cause lighter tan or yellowish-brown spots that spread rapidly across both old and new leaves. The spots often look wet or translucent around the borders, and leaves fall off while parts of the leaf are still light green.
* **Corrective step:** Prune off affected leaves immediately using sterile shears, wiping the blades with rubbing alcohol between every single cut. Isolate the plant from other houseplants to stop airborne or water-borne transmission, and avoid splashing water onto the leaves when watering.

## Diagnostic Quick Reference

* **Green, firm leaf dropped:** Environmental shock or drafts. Relocate away from cold air streams.
* **Green, limp leaf dropped:** Hydrophobic, dry soil. Perform a deep bottom-soak.
* **Dark brown/black spots (center/base):** Root rot. Trim bad roots and reduce watering frequency.
* **Fine brown speckling:** Spider mites. Clean leaves and apply insecticidal soap.
