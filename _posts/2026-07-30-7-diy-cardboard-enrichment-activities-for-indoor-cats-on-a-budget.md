Indoor cats need daily mental and physical stimulation to thrive. Without structured play, bored cats often develop behavioral issues like excessive vocalization, night crazies, or destructive scratching. Fortunately, you do not need expensive electronic gadgets to keep your feline engaged. Household cardboard—from shipping boxes to paper towel tubes—is non-toxic, lightweight, and perfect for building low-cost toys that tap into a cat's natural hunting and [environmental enrichment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_enrichment) needs. Here are 7 distinct DIY cardboard enrichment activities you can assemble in under 15 minutes.

## 1. The Pyramid Treat Wall

* **Materials needed:** 6 to 10 empty toilet paper rolls, non-toxic liquid glue or masking tape, dry kibble or treats.
* **Assembly:** Stack the cardboard tubes horizontally into a pyramid shape (4 on the bottom, 3 in the middle, 2, then 1 on top). Glue or tape the sides of the tubes together where they meet. Once dry, place the pyramid on its side so the open tube ends face outward. 
* **How it works:** Drop a few pieces of high-value treats into random tubes. Your cat will have to hook their paws inside to drag the food out, encouraging problem-solving and dexterity.

## 2. Whack-a-Mole Shoebox

* **Materials needed:** A cardboard shoebox with a fitted lid, a utility knife, 2–3 ping pong balls or lightweight cat springs.
* **Assembly:** Use the utility knife to cut 5 to 7 circular holes (about 1.5 to 2 inches in diameter) across the top lid of the box. Drop ping pong balls or small jingle balls inside, then tape the lid securely closed.
* **How it works:** When your cat swats at a ball through one hole, it rolls across the bottom and pops up under a different hole, triggering their natural predatory instincts.

## 3. Egg Carton Slow-Feeder

* **Materials needed:** Clean 12-count cardboard egg carton, dry cat food, 3–4 crumpled paper balls.
* **Assembly:** Open the egg carton and scatter half a meal's worth of kibble across the 12 individual cups. To increase the difficulty, roll small pieces of scrap paper or tissue into light balls and place them over a few of the cups.
* **How it works:** This forces your cat to use their nose and paws to remove obstacles before reaching the food, simulating natural [foraging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foraging) behavior.

## 4. Vertical Tissue Box Puzzle

* **Materials needed:** An empty rectangular tissue box, 5–6 toilet paper rolls cut in half.
* **Assembly:** Cut 5 or 6 toilet paper rolls in half crosswise so they stand about 2 to 3 inches tall. Pack these cut tubes vertically into the top plastic opening of the empty tissue box until they fit snugly without falling over.
* **How it works:** Drop treats into the open tops of the vertical cylinders. Your cat must fish the treats up and out using single claws, which slows down fast eaters and provides focused mental work.

## 5. Corrugated Scratching Block

* **Materials needed:** Medium shipping box, ruler, utility knife, non-toxic craft glue.
* **Assembly:** Cut cardboard into uniform strips measuring 2 inches wide by 10 inches long. Make sure the exposed corrugated edges (the zig-zag interior layer) face upward. Glue the flat sides of the strips together, pressing them tightly until you build a block that is 6 to 8 inches wide. Let it dry completely.
* **How it works:** Sprinkling a pinch of catnip over the exposed grooves makes this a cheap, renewable alternative to commercial scratch pads that saves your furniture from claws.

## 6. Box-in-a-Box Tunnel Maze

* **Materials needed:** One large shipping box, one small box, box cutter, packing tape.
* **Assembly:** Cut archways (roughly 4 inches tall) into opposite sides of the smaller box. Place the small box inside the large box and tape it to the center floor. Cut several 3-inch paw holes along the outer walls of the large box.
* **How it works:** Drop a noisy ball or catnip mouse inside the inner chamber. Your cat can reach through the outer walls to bat the toy through the inner archways, extending play time.

## 7. Hanging Cardboard Swat Toy

* **Materials needed:** Sturdy corrugated cardboard, scissors, jute twine, door handle or cat tree arm.
* **Assembly:** Cut 3 or 4 cardboard shapes—such as 3-inch long feather or fish outlines. Poke a small hole near the top of each piece and thread a length of natural jute twine through, knotting it securely. Tie the free ends of the strings to a door handle at your cat's chest height.
* **How it works:** As your cat bats them, the lightweight cardboard swings unpredictably, giving them a safe, silent target for solitary play while you are away.
