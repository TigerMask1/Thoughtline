That heavy, lingering feeling on Sunday late afternoon—often dubbed the [Sunday Scaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunday_Scaries)—isn't just random stress. It is usually your brain's response to open loops: unfinished tasks from last week mixed with an unorganized pile of demands waiting for you on Monday. 

Instead of letting that worry run in the background, set aside 15 minutes every Sunday evening to work through these 15 weekly review journal prompts. They are divided into three distinct phases: reviewing where your energy went, clearing out current anxiety, and setting actionable intentions for Monday.

## Phase 1: Close Last Week's Loops

Before you can focus on next week, you need to mentally close the door on the past seven days. 

1. **What were my top three wins from this week, regardless of size?**
   *Example: Finishing a draft report early, cooking three home meals, or sticking to a bedtime.*  
2. **What single task or situation drained my energy the most?**
   *Identifying the exact leak prevents you from repeating the same schedule mistake next week.*  
3. **What open task is still hanging over my head, and what is its immediate next action?**
   *Break a vague stressor ("finish taxes") into a 5-minute action ("find 1099 form").*  
4. **When did I feel most present or relaxed over the last seven days?**
   *Note the environment, people, or habits present during that moment so you can replicate them.*  
5. **What did last week teach me about my current workload or boundary limits?**
   *Extract a clear takeaway instead of just feeling overwhelmed.*  

## Phase 2: Process Sunday Anxiety

Unspoken anxiety grows in the dark. Writing down your exact fears strips them of their power and moves your brain from threat detection into problem-solving, a core concept behind popular productivity frameworks like [Getting Things Done](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Getting_Things_Done).

6. **What specific event or meeting next week is creating tension in my body right now?**
   *Name the exact calendar event rather than letting a general sense of dread linger.*  
7. **What is the absolute worst-case scenario my mind is imagining for that situation?**
   *Writing it down usually reveals how unlikely or manageable that scenario actually is.*  
8. **What aspects of next week are entirely within my control, and what must I accept?**
   *Draw two columns on your page: "My Control" vs. "Out of My Control."*
9. **What is one meeting, request, or distraction I need to say "no" to this week?**
   *Protect your schedule before someone else fills it for you.*  
10. **What does my body need right now to transition smoothly into rest tonight?**
    *Whether it's a 20-minute walk, turning off your phone at 8 PM, or stretching.*  

## Phase 3: Set Monday Intentions

Finish your journaling session by mapping out clarity for Monday morning so you wake up with a strategy rather than a sense of panic.

11. **If I could only accomplish one core objective by Friday, what would make this week a success?**
    *Define your single priority before opening your inbox.*  
12. **What micro-task will I complete in my first 30 minutes on Monday to build instant momentum?**
    *Keep it small: clear three specific emails, review a document, or outline an agenda.*  
13. **What three non-negotiable personal commitments am I scheduling for myself?**
    *Block time for workouts, family dinners, or reading just like you would a work meeting.*  
14. **Who is one person I can reach out to, support, or thank this week?**
    *Shifting focus toward connection reduces internal work stress.*  
15. **What single word defines the mindset I want to bring to Monday morning?**
    *Examples: "Patience," "Focus," "Calm," or "Thorough." Write this word at the top of your Monday to-do list.*  

## How to Keep This Routine Under 15 Minutes

You do not need to write long essays for every prompt. Set a kitchen timer for 15 minutes, grab a plain notebook, and answer each prompt in 1–2 bullet points. 

If 15 prompts feels overwhelming on your first try, pick just one prompt from each phase (3 total). The goal isn't perfect prose; it's emptying your brain onto paper so you can enjoy your Sunday evening and step into Monday with quiet confidence.
