Hosting an outdoor [potluck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potluck) on a tight budget forces you to make practical trade-offs. One of the biggest decisions comes down to drink service: do you go with batch beverage dispensers or set up an ice tub filled with canned and bottled drinks? Both options have fanatical defenders, but when every dollar and effort-hour counts, the right choice depends on your crowd size, menu, and tolerance for prep work.

## Option 1: Beverage Dispensers (Maximum Savings, Higher Effort)

A couple of 1.5- to 2-gallon glass or plastic dispensers (often $15 to $25 each at big-box retailers or thrift stores) allow you to serve homemade drinks like iced tea, lemonade, or spa-style cucumber water.

**The Math:** A 2-gallon dispenser holds roughly twenty-five 10-ounce servings. Making two gallons of homemade iced tea costs under $2 (a dozen black tea bags, a cup of sugar, and tap water). Compare that to buying two 12-packs of canned soda, which runs $12 to $18 depending on sales.

**Pros:**
- **Extremely low cost per serving:** Homemade tea, punch, or infused water costs pennies per glass.
- **Minimal trash:** Paired with paper cups or reusable plastic tumblers, you won't spend the evening clearing away crushed aluminum cans.
- **Visual appeal:** Clear dispensers layered with sliced lemons, mint, or berries instantly elevate a picnic table.

**Cons:**
- **Dilution risk:** Adding ice directly into the dispenser dilutes the drink as it melts. To avoid watery lemonade, freeze batch mix or fruit slices into ice blocks instead of using plain ice cubes.
- **Clogging and spigot leaks:** Pulpy juices or floating fruit can clog standard spigots, creating sticky messes and slow lines.
- **Prep and clean-up time:** You have to wash the container, mix liquids beforehand, transport them without spilling, and scrub spigot parts afterward.

## Option 2: Ice Tubs (Ultimate Convenience, Higher Cost)

An ice tub setup uses a simple [galvanized steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galvanization) tub, a plastic storage tote, or a flexible utility bucket ($10 to $20 at hardware stores) packed with 15 to 20 pounds of bagged ice ($5 to $8 total).

**The Math:** A standard 15-gallon tub holds roughly 35 to 40 12-ounce cans along with two 10-pound bags of ice. If you buy three 12-packs of soda or sparkling water, your drink cost is around $20, plus $6 for ice, bringing total beverage overhead to ~$26.

**Pros:**
- **Zero prep work:** Open the bags of ice, dump in the cans, and walk away.
- **Great for BYOB:** An ice tub naturally invites guests to contribute their own favorite canned beverages to the communal stash.
- **Maximum chill:** Submerging cans in an ice-water slurry cools drinks down to 32–35°F faster than any dispenser, without diluting the beverage inside.

**Cons:**
- **Soggy labels:** Paper labels on bottles turn to mush in melted ice water. Stick to aluminum cans or drinks with printed graphics.
- **Trash accumulation:** You must set up dedicated recycling bags right next to the tub, or cans will wind up scattered across the yard.
- **Weight:** A tub loaded with 40 cans and 20 pounds of ice weighs over 50 pounds—place it on a sturdy table or bench before filling it.

## Cost and Logistics Breakdown for 20 Guests

Assuming an average potluck duration of 3 hours, plan on 2 to 3 drink servings per person (40–60 total drinks).

*   **Dispenser Route:** Buying one 2-gallon dispenser ($18), ingredients for iced tea and lemonade ($5), and 60 paper cups ($6) totals around **$29** for your first event. That drops to **$11** for every subsequent potluck once you own the dispenser.
*   **Ice Tub Route:** Buying one 15-gallon tub ($18), two bags of ice ($6), and 48 cans of soda/seltzer ($20) totals around **$44** for your first event, and **$26** per event thereafter.

## The Verdict: Which Should You Choose?

Choose **beverage dispensers** if you are hosting on a strict budget, want a low-waste event, and don't mind spending 20 minutes in the kitchen pre-mixing drinks before guests arrive.

Choose an **ice tub** if convenience is your top priority, your potluck is BYOB-friendly, or you lack the kitchen prep space to batch drinks.

**The Budget Hybrid Strategy:** If you want the best of both worlds, set up one simple 2-gallon dispenser filled with free ice water and lemon slices alongside a small ice tub for guests who bring their own canned drinks. This keeps your host costs under $10 while ensuring everyone stays hydrated.
