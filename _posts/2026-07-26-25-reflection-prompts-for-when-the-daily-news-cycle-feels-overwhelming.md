Constantly checking breaking news feeds can trigger high levels of stress, a phenomenon often referred to as [doomscrolling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doomscrolling). When headline stress builds up, your nervous system responds as if you are in immediate personal danger, even if the events are happening thousands of miles away. Instead of turning off your awareness completely, reflection prompts offer a structured way to step back, process your emotions, and regain perspective.

Here are 25 targeted journaling and reflection prompts organized by category to help you navigate news fatigue and re-establish healthy boundaries with media.

## Assessing Your Current Emotional State

1. What physical sensations (muscle tension, shallow breathing, clenched jaw) am I experiencing right now while reading the news?
2. Which specific story or topic today triggered the strongest emotional reaction, and why did it hit so hard?
3. Am I looking at the news right now out of a genuine desire to stay informed, or as an anxious habit to fill empty time?
4. What three words best describe my current mood after closing my news apps?
5. If I stepped away from all media for the next 24 hours, what fear arises about what I might miss? Is that fear realistic?
6. How has my energy level changed from the moment I woke up to after my morning news scroll?

## Setting Healthy Consumption Boundaries

7. What specific time of day is worst for my news consumption (such as right before bed or first thing in the morning), and what can I replace that slot with?
8. Which platforms or media outlets leave me feeling informed, and which ones leave me feeling panicked or outraged?
9. What is a realistic daily time limit for reading news (e.g., 15 minutes twice a day), and what tool can I use to enforce it?
10. Am I engaging in discussions in comment sections or group chats that drain my energy without producing positive outcomes?
11. Who in my life can I talk to about current events without falling into spiral conversations?
12. What rule can I create for myself regarding push notifications on my phone?

## Identifying Your Locus of Control

13. Out of all the issues worrying me in the news today, which ones can I directly influence, and which are completely outside my control?
14. What is one small, practical action I can take this week (such as donating $10, volunteering an hour, or writing a letter) to support a cause I care about?
15. How can I shift my focus from global outcomes to local, neighborhood-level needs where my efforts have a visible impact?
16. What strengths or resources do I possess right now that allow me to remain resilient during unpredictable times?
17. Am I confusing "staying informed" with "carrying the weight of the world"? Where can I give myself permission to set the weight down?
18. What positive, constructive work is happening right now behind the headlines that isn't getting broadcasted?

## Grounding in the Immediate Present

19. What are five tangible things in my immediate room right now that bring me comfort or stability?
20. Who is someone in my immediate life who needs my attention, support, or presence today?
21. What non-digital activity can I do for 30 minutes today that completely absorbs my focus (e.g., cooking, gardening, drawing)?
22. What is a good thing that happened in my personal life this past week that had nothing to do with global events?
23. What routine or habit keeps me feeling steady when external circumstances feel chaotic?
24. How can I practice [mindfulness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindfulness) during my next meal or walk to anchor myself in the present moment?
25. What promise can I make to myself today about how I will treat my mind and body when news fatigue sets in again?

## How to Use These Prompts Effectively

Don't try to answer all 25 prompts at once—that will only add to your overwhelm. Pick just one or two prompts that resonate with your immediate state of mind. Set a timer for 5 to 10 minutes, grab a notebook, and write continuously without editing your thoughts. Returning to this practice regularly will help you maintain a healthy relationship with current events without sacrificing your mental well-being.
