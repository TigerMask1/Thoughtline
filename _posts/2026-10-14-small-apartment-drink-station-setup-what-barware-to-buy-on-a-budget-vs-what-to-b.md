Setting up a home bar when you live in 600 square feet is mostly an exercise in ruthless spatial editing. You do not have room for a freestanding mahogany cart, five types of single-purpose glassware, or novelty gadgets that only make one drink. 

A functional, high-output drink station takes up about 12 by 16 inches—roughly the size of a standard serving tray on top of a bookshelf, dresser, or kitchen counter. To keep costs low and shelves clear, divide your gear into three categories: workhorse tools worth owning, bulky event items you should borrow, and kitchen utensils you already have.

## What to Buy: The $50 Everyday Core

Invest in durable, unbranded stainless steel for the tools you will touch every week. Avoid pre-packaged "12-piece bar sets" from department stores; they typically bundle cheap, thin metal with items you will never touch.

* **A set of weighted Boston shaker tins ($12–$18):** Skip three-piece cobbler shakers with the built-in cap. The metal contracts when chilled, freezing the cap in place and making it nearly impossible to open mid-drink. A two-piece metal-on-metal [cocktail shaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocktail_shaker) (an 18 oz tin paired with a 28 oz tin) creates a reliable seal, chills liquids instantly, and separates cleanly with a light tap on the side.
* **A Japanese-style jigger ($10–$14):** Eyeballing pours leads to unbalanced drinks and wasted liquor. A two-sided Japanese jigger (typically 1 oz on one side, 2 oz on the other) with interior etched lines for 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, and 1.5 ounces gives you complete precision without taking up space.
* **A tight-coil Hawthorne strainer ($8–$12):** This sits over your shaker tin to hold back cracked ice and citrus pulp. Look for one with a dense spring coil; looser coils let small ice chips through, muddying the texture of a stirred or shaken drink.
* **A 30 cm spiral bar spoon ($6–$8):** Stirring a cocktail with a standard teaspoon is awkward and splashes liquid. A long, weighted bar spoon glides along the perimeter of a mixing vessel to chill drinks quietly without chipping ice.
* **One set of Double Old Fashioned (DOF) glasses:** If you only own one style of glassware, make it a 10-to-12-ounce heavy-bottomed lowball glass. It holds neat pours, drinks on the rocks, Negronis, sours, and doubles as your everyday water glass.

## What to Borrow for Gatherings

Certain items are only relevant when you host more than four people. Because they consume massive amounts of cabinet space the other 360 days of the year, borrow these from family or neighbors instead of buying them:

* **Punch bowls and beverage dispensers:** A glass 2-gallon drink dispenser is a nightmare to store in an apartment. When hosting a party, ask a friend to lend you theirs, or simply mix a batch of drinks in a 2-quart pitcher or clean growlers stored in the fridge.
* **Specialty stemware:** Coupes, martini glasses, highball glasses, and champagne flutes look great, but they are fragile and difficult to stack. For casual group hosting, borrow extra glassware from a neighbor, or pick up a six-pack of basic stemless wine glasses from a thrift store for a few dollars.
* **Ice buckets and coolers:** High-capacity ice chests belong in garages, not apartment closets. Borrow a small cooler or insulated bucket the morning of an event to keep excess ice cold off-counter.

## Tools to Repurpose from Your Kitchen

Before spending money on cocktail-specific accessories, raid your cutlery drawer. Several common culinary tools outperform their bar-specific counterparts.

* **Vegetable peeler instead of a channel knife:** A simple Y-peeler, like the classic model from [OXO](https://www.oxo.com), cuts broad, clean citrus swaths with just the right amount of flavorful oil and minimal bitter white pith.
* **A French rolling pin instead of a muddler:** The flat end of a wooden rolling pin or the base of a heavy wooden spoon crushes mint, berries, and sugar cubes just as effectively as an $18 custom muddler.
* **A fine-mesh tea strainer:** If you want crystal-clear shaken drinks without stray herb flecks or ice shards, pass the drink through a small kitchen tea strainer held directly above the glass.

## Keeping the Footprint Small

Organize your essentials onto a single rimmed tray. Place your shaker, jigger, strainer, and spoon at the back corner, with three to four foundational spirits and a bottle of aromatic bitters in front. When you need the counter space back for meal prep, you can pick up the entire station in one motion and shift it to the dining table.
