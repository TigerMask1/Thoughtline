Hosting a game night for six to eight players is notoriously tricky, especially when your roster mixes hardcore board gamers with people who would rather play charades. At this player count, team-based deduction games are your best bet, and the two undisputed titans in the category are [Codenames](https://czechgames.com/en/codenames/) and [Decrypto on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/225694/decrypto).

Both split your table into two competing teams, test your associative thinking, and cost under thirty dollars. Yet they play out completely differently at the table. Here is how they stack up across the friction points that define a mixed-skill game night.

## The Teach: Simplicity vs. Initial Confusion

If you have casual players who glaze over during rule explanations, Codenames wins comfortably.

You lay out a 5x5 grid of twenty-five word cards. One "Spymaster" per team looks at a hidden key card and gives a one-word, one-number clue (like "Bark: 2" to connect *Dog* and *Tree*). Their teammates guess the matching cards while avoiding the other team's cards and the instant-loss "assassin." You can explain the rules in two minutes, and everyone understands how to play within thirty seconds of the first turn.

Decrypto, by contrast, sounds unnecessarily complicated when explained aloud. Each team has four numbered screen slots with hidden keywords (for instance: 1. Apple, 2. Ocean, 3. Paris, 4. Hammer). An Encryptor draws a three-digit code like 3-1-4 and must write three clues that match those keywords so their teammates can decode the number—all while trying to prevent the opposing team from intercepting the code in future rounds.

Explaining the interception sheet, the difference between guessing words versus guessing numbers, and why you don't reveal your keywords until the end often leads to blank stares. Decrypto always requires a throwaway practice round before casual players experience the "aha!" moment.

## The Hot Seat: Who Carries the Mental Load?

In Codenames, the burden sits squarely on the two Spymasters. If a casual player or someone prone to analysis paralysis gets cast as the Spymaster, the game can stall hard. Staring at twenty-five disconnected nouns while trying to find a clever link between *Platypus*, *Drill*, and *Embassy* without accidentally hinting at the assassin card is genuinely stressful. If your mixed-skill group has quiet or anxious players, they will almost always refuse to be the Spymaster, leaving the role to the same two experienced gamers game after game.

Decrypto distributes the pressure far better. The Encryptor role rotates every round, meaning every person at the table takes a turn giving clues. Writing three individual clues for specific words is mentally easier than weaving a multi-word net across a sprawling grid. If your word is *Apple*, writing *Cider* or *Newton* is approachable for anyone, regardless of gaming experience.

## Downtime and Table Engagement at 6 to 8 Players

Player engagement is where Decrypto pulls ahead significantly, particularly with larger groups.

In Codenames, when the Red Spymaster is thinking, nobody else has anything active to do. With six to eight players, this creates extended dead air where four to six people check their phones. When a team finally makes their guesses, the opposing team merely sits back and hopes for a miss.

Decrypto has virtually zero downtime because rounds are played concurrently. While the Encryptors write their clues, both teams are actively chatting, reviewing past clues on their deduction sheets, and trying to reverse-engineer the other team's hidden words. When Team A gives their clues, Team B doesn't just wait; they actively try to intercept the code first. Every player remains engaged on every single turn.

## The Verdict

Choose **Codenames** if:
- More than half the room rarely plays modern tabletop games.
- You want a relaxed, social atmosphere where people can jump in and out to grab a drink without pausing the game.
- You have two confident, willing players ready to shoulder the Spymaster duties so the rest of the table can enjoy low-stress guessing.

Choose **Decrypto** if:
- Your players are willing to struggle through a five-minute explanation and one clumsy practice round.
- You want to eliminate long stretches of silence and phone-checking.
- You prefer a game where everyone takes an equal turn in the hot seat and every turn requires input from both sides of the table.
