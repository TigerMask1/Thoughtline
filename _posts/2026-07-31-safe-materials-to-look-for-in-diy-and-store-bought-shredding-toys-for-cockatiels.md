Cockatiels and small-to-medium conures (like Green-Cheeked and Sun Conures) have an innate drive to chew, shred, and forage. Providing the right shredding toys keeps their beaks trimmed, prevents boredom, and helps prevent feather-picking. However, because birds ingest tiny particles or wrap threads around their toes, selecting safe materials is critical. Whether buying commercial toys or assembling DIY toys at home, stick to these proven, bird-safe materials.

## Safe Papers, Cardboards, and Sheeting

Paper products are among the cheapest and most satisfying shredding mediums for cockatiels and conures, as both species prefer thin, easy-to-tear textures.

- **Unbleached Kraft Paper & Brown Paper Bags:** Standard brown shipping paper or unbleached packing paper makes excellent shredding material. Cut it into 1/2-inch to 1-inch strips, weave it through cage bars, or crumple it around seeds to create instant foraging packages. Avoid glossy printed paper, glossy coupons, or paper with metallic inks, as heavy metal residues and plastic coatings can be toxic.
- **Corrugated Cardboard Strips & Sleeves:** Plain, clean corrugated cardboard (like coffee cup sleeves or clean shipping boxes without heavy printing or plastic tape) provides a satisfying crunch. Avoid pizza boxes or food containers with grease stains, food residue, or scented laundry detergent exposure.
- **Coffee Filters:** Natural, unbleached paper coffee filters make fantastic accordion-style shredders. Stack 5 to 10 filters, punch a hole through the center with a hole punch, thread a paper rope through them, and knot the bottom for an instant DIY shredding flower.
- **Crinkle Paper Shred:** Uncolored or vegetable-dyed crinkle paper is standard in foraging toys. Ensure store-bought crinkle paper uses non-toxic, water-soluble dyes rather than solvent-based industrial inks.

## Soft Woods and Pith Fibers

Hardwoods like oak or maple are too tough for small conure and cockatiel beaks. Target soft woods and pithy plant materials that break apart easily under light beak pressure.

- **Balsa Wood:** Extremely soft and lightweight, [balsa wood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ochroma_pyramidale) is ideal for cockatiels and smaller conures. Soft slats measuring 1/16-inch to 1/8-inch thick allow smaller birds to snap off pieces easily, building their confidence.
- **Yucca (Sol-D-Knot Material):** Dried yucca stalk discs (commonly sold as Bird Kabobs) feature a soft, fibrous center surrounded by a slightly tougher outer ring. Conures peel the outer ring while cockatiels shred the spongy interior.
- **Sola Wood:** Made from the inner pith of the sola plant, sola balls and sola logs are paper-soft and break into lightweight flakes. They are completely safe if ingested in small amounts during chewing.
- **Kiln-Dried White Pine:** For conures with slightly stronger beaks, 1/4-inch to 1/2-inch thick kiln-dried pine slats offer a firmer crunch. Always ensure pine is kiln-dried to remove excess sap and volatile resins. Never use pressure-treated lumber, which contains chemical preservatives like copper.

## Safe Ropes, Fasteners, and Hardware

How you tie or hang a shredding toy is just as important as the toy itself. Loose threads can snag toes or cause crop impactions, and zinc-coated hardware poses a severe heavy-metal toxicity risk.

- **Untreated Paper Rope:** The single safest stringing material for shredders. If a bird chews through paper twine, digested fibers break down easily without causing intestinal blockages.
- **Natural Sisal and Untreated Hemp:** High-grade, untreated [sisal fiber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sisal) rope and pure hemp twine are strong enough for multi-piece toys. Avoid ropes treated with mineral oils or chemical lubricants—look specifically for unoiled or food-safe strands. Keep exposed loop lengths under 2 inches to prevent entanglement.
- **Vegetable-Tanned Leather Strips:** Vegetable-tanned leather strips (free from chromium salts and chemical dyes) are great for stringing wood blocks and paper coils.
- **Stainless Steel Hardware:** Always use 304 or 316-grade stainless steel quick links (pear links or C-clamps) to hang toys. Avoid zinc-plated, galvanized, or brass hardware. Never use split key rings, as beaks and toe-nails can easily get pinched in the split wire.

## DIY Assembly Safety Tips

When building toys at home, follow these quick rules:

1. **Avoid Commercial Glues:** Use mechanical joining (drilling holes, knotting paper twine, or weaving through cardboard) rather than adhesives. Avoid hot glue, superglue, and standard craft glues.
2. **Size Appropriately:** Cut paper strips wide enough so they don't form ultra-thin, thread-like strings that can wrap tightly around a bird's tongue or toes.
3. **Inspect Daily:** Trim frayed rope ends daily. Discard shredded paper and cardboard once it becomes soiled with droppings or damp from water dishes to prevent fungal and bacterial growth.
